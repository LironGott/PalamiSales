# Sales deck: market research notes (2026-10-15)

## What works in a B2B sales-meeting deck
- Open with a change in the world, not with "you have a problem" (Andy Raskin, strategic narrative): the shift, who wins and who loses, the promised land, features as "gifts" that get past obstacles, proof.
- Discovery is a conversation, not slides. Gong (803,402 meetings, 2021) found slides during discovery correlate with fewer follow-up meetings. Ask 3–4 questions first, then reflect the answers back.
- About 8–12 focused slides. End with a concrete next step (a pilot with a date), not "Questions?".
- ROI: calculate it with the prospect's own numbers. Do not use invented benchmarks.

## Citable stats (US surveys, flag as such)
- Travelport Digital Traveler Survey, 2018: 64–67% want the whole itinerary in one place.
- ASTA "How America Travels", 2018 (sponsored by Carnival): 35% use an advisor to have someone to help if travel is disrupted; 39% for the level of service.
- Contiki Next Gen Travel Trends, 2025: only 7% of Gen Z would let AI book their whole trip.
- No credible data exists for:
  - hours advisors spend on documents
  - repeat or referral share
  - loyalty lift from a branded app
- Do not claim the items above. Measure them in pilots instead.

## Competitor messaging (Travefy, Vamoos, Umapped, AXUS, Tern, TravelJoy)
- Common claims:
  - a branded app with the agency logo
  - save hours of data entry
  - the client has everything offline
  - stay present between booking and return
  - loyalty and the next booking
  - the advisor stays in control
